How to use it

  1. Enter your invite code
  2. Agree to sharing (start and end stay private)
  3. Pick a nickname and where the phone rides
  4. Allow location and notifications
  5. Add your bike: specs load by themselves
  6. Mount the phone, tap Start recording, ride
  7. Road alerts warn you before breakers and potholes
  8. After the ride: your review, deep analysis and a ride card
  9. Tell us what you think from Profile → Beta feedback
Why it exists

Built for Indian roads

Speed breakers without paint or signs, potholes that move every monsoon, and stretches of broken road that wreck rims and backs. Navigation apps tell you where to turn, but nothing warns you what the road ahead is like, or tells you how well you rode it.

Riding apps that do score you use one rule for every bike, so a scooter and an adventure bike are judged the same way. Dashcam-style apps heat the phone up within minutes on a handlebar.

Tango Ride Club is a cool-running recorder that learns the road from real rides, warns you before the bad bits, and gives a fair, practical review for the bike you ride. Every rider in the club adds to the road map.

New · road alerts

Know what's coming

Once a speed breaker, pothole or rough patch has shown up on two or more rides, the app warns you about 7 seconds before you reach it: a short voice line through your phone or helmet headset, and one big, calm picture on the screen, even with the screen locked. It shows one distance, stays about 4 seconds, and fades away. No flashing, no countdown to stare at.

Record

One button, then ride

The app uses only the phone's motion sensors and GPS (no camera), so it stays cool on the handlebar for a whole commute or a highway day.

  • Records with the screen off or while Google Maps is on top
  • Warns you if battery saver would switch GPS off
  • "Start & open Google Maps" for navigation in one tap
  • "Share my ride with family": Google Maps live location, or a "Starting my ride" message
After the ride

A review, not just numbers

Each ride gets a score out of 100, what you did well, up to three tips with a Street View link to the exact spot, and a comparison with your last ride on the same route.

  • Braking for a speed breaker counts as good riding, not hard braking
  • Cornering lean from real bends only, not junction turns
  • Rough road, bumpy stretches and big hits, climb and descent
  • Rides are named automatically ("Home → Office") and repeat routes are grouped
Deep analysis

The route, km by km

  • Speed, bumps and roughness for every km, and how long each took compared with usual
  • Known bumps: spots felt on most rides here, so they're real speed breakers or potholes
  • What the weather did: heat, rain, extra drag from a headwind
  • What it meant for your bike: fuel and cost, range left, service due "in N rides like this"
Share

A ride card worth posting

Open any ride, tap ⋯ → Share ride card, and post it to WhatsApp Status, Instagram or a chat, or save it to your gallery.

  • Street map, terrain map, or just the route shape (the most private)
  • Up to 6 numbers from 28: distance, top speed, 0-100 km/h, max lean, climb, smoothness, breakers and more
  • A one-line summary from the ride's own numbers. Keep it, rewrite it, or turn it off.
  • Your nickname and bike at the top, or hide the nickname with one tap
My bike

Judged for the bike you ride

Type the model ("fasc", "pulsar", "himalayan") and its specs load by themselves from about 220 Indian bikes and scooters. Every ride is then judged by that bike's own rules.

  • Fuel log, mileage, cost per km and range left
  • Service and chain-lube countdowns, insurance and PUC reminders
  • Several bikes, with rides kept separate per bike

Health data will stay on your phone. It won't be shared with the club unless a separate, clearly worded option is added and you turn it on.

Privacy

What leaves the phone

Shared, only with your OK

  • Shared: ride recordings with the first and last 200 m cut off, the road spots your phone learned (none near where rides start or end), bike model and type, phone model, your nickname
  • Never: your name, photo, licence, papers, fuel or service records, or your Home and Office places
  • Switch sharing off, or choose Wi-Fi only, any time in Profile

Why share at all

Each ride shows where the speed breakers, potholes and rough stretches really are. Pooled together, those rides make the road alerts and reviews better for every rider in the club.

Invite-only beta

Got an invite? Install it here

Tango Ride Club is being tested by a small group of riders first. If a rider in the club gave you an invite code, enter it to get the app. No code yet? Ask the rider who told you about it.

Download the app
  1. Enter your code and tap Download on your Android phone. Chrome may say the file can harm your device: that's its standard message for any app from outside the Play Store. Choose Download anyway.
  2. Open the downloaded file. If Android asks, allow installs from Chrome, then tap Install.
  3. Open Tango Ride Club, enter the same invite code, and follow the setup: agree to sharing, pick a nickname, choose where the phone rides, allow location and notifications, add your bike.
  4. Mount the phone on the handlebar, tap Start recording, and ride. Tell us how it went from Profile → Beta feedback.

Android 11 or newer with GPS. Keep battery saver off while riding, or GPS stops when the screen locks. New versions are announced inside the app; your rides stay when you update. Phones locked by a finance or work policy can't install apps from outside the Play Store.
